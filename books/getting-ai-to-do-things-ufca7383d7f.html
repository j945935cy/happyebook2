Getting AI to Do Things

A Practical Guide to Clear Instructions and Reliable Workflows

AI can produce a polished answer and still miss the point, fill in details you never supplied, or give you something that is difficult to use. The answer is not to search for a magic prompt. It is to make the task clear, provide the right context and source material, set useful constraints, and decide in advance what a good deliverable must include. This practical guide introduces a six-part task brief: goal, context, source material, constraints, deliverable, and acceptance criteria. Through examples and exercises, it shows how to choose suitable tasks, handle missing information, break larger work into manageable stages, give specific feedback, check important claims, and build repeatable workflows. The book also explains why a fluent answer is not proof of accuracy, why sensitive data and third-party material require care, and why external or high-stakes actions need appropriate human approval. Its examples are teaching illustrations—not evidence that a particular AI tool will always follow instructions or produce a correct result. Written for general readers using AI in work, learning, and everyday tasks, the book assumes no programming background. It does not promise that any prompt guarantees correctness or that AI replaces human judgment. AI output may be incomplete or wrong; people remain responsible for decisions, and specialist questions should go to qualified professionals.
